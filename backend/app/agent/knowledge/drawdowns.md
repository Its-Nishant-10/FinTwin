## What a drawdown is

A drawdown is the fall in value from a previous peak. The maximum drawdown is the largest such fall over a period. It measures the worst loss an investor who bought at the top would have sat through, which matters more to most people than average volatility.

## 2008 global financial crisis in India

During the 2008 global financial crisis the Nifty 50 fell by roughly 60% from its January 2008 peak to its low in October 2008. It took roughly two to three years to regain the previous peak. Investors who kept investing through the fall bought units at much lower prices.

## 2020 COVID-19 crash in India

In early 2020 the Nifty 50 fell by roughly 38% between its January high and its low in late March 2020. It regained its previous high by around November 2020, one of the fastest recoveries on record for the index.

## Why recovery time matters

A fall of 30% needs a gain of about 43% to get back to where it started, because the recovery is measured from a lower base. How long a recovery takes varies widely between episodes, which is why FinTwin models a crash both as a permanent loss and with an optional recovery period.
