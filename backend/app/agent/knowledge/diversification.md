## Diversification

Diversification means spreading money across holdings whose prices do not all move together. When one holding falls, others may not, so the portfolio as a whole swings less than its parts. The benefit comes from low correlation between holdings, not from the number of holdings alone.

## Concentration and the Herfindahl-Hirschman Index

The Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) of a portfolio is the sum of the squared weights of its holdings. It is 1 when everything is in a single holding and 1/n when money is split equally across n holdings. Its inverse, 1/HHI, is the effective number of holdings: a portfolio of 20 stocks with an effective number of 3 behaves roughly like 3 equally sized positions.

## Sector concentration

Holding many different stocks from the same sector gives less diversification than the count suggests, because stocks in one sector tend to be affected by the same news, regulation and business cycle. Checking allocation by sector, not only by stock, shows this.
