## Roles of the main asset classes

Equity offers the highest long-run growth potential and the largest swings. Debt (bonds, debt funds) offers lower, steadier returns and cushions falls in equity. Gold has historically moved differently from equities and is often held as a diversifier. Cash is stable in nominal terms but tends to lose purchasing power to inflation over long periods.

## Changing the equity-debt mix

Moving money from equity to debt usually lowers both the expected return and the volatility of a portfolio. Whether that helps a goal depends on the time horizon: over short horizons lower volatility reduces the chance of a bad outcome, while over long horizons the lower expected return can reduce the typical outcome.

## FinTwin's asset-class assumptions

FinTwin's allocation scenarios use round-number, illustrative long-run assumptions for each asset class, for example 12% expected return and 18% volatility for equity and 7% and 4% for debt. They are not forecasts and are not fitted to market data; they exist so that the current and a hypothetical allocation are compared on the same basis.
