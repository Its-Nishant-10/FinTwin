## What a SIP is

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) invests a fixed amount into a mutual fund at a regular interval, usually monthly. It is a method of investing, not an investment product in itself: the returns depend entirely on the fund the money goes into.

## Rupee-cost averaging

Because a SIP invests the same rupee amount every month, it buys more units when prices are low and fewer when prices are high. Over a volatile period this lowers the average cost per unit compared with the average price. It does not guarantee a profit and does not protect against losses in a falling market.

## Pausing or stopping a SIP

Many fund houses let investors pause a SIP for a limited number of months. A pause does not sell existing units; it only stops new purchases. The cost of a pause is the growth the skipped instalments would have earned, and it is largest when the pause coincides with low prices, because those are the instalments that would have bought the most units.

## SIP versus lump sum

A lump sum puts all the money to work at once, so it benefits most from a rising market and suffers most from an early fall. A SIP spreads the entry over time, reducing the risk of investing everything just before a decline. Neither approach wins in every market; the difference depends on the path prices take.
